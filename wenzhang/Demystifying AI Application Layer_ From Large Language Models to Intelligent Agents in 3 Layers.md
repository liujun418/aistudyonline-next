# Demystifying AI Application Layer: From Large Language Models to Intelligent Agents in 3 Layers

Are you only using AI as a chat tool or a more intelligent browser? Do you still struggle to understand what AI large models, intelligent agents, and AI workflows really are? This guide will break down the underlying logic of the AI application layer in the most accessible way, even for complete AI beginners\. By the end, you’ll have a fresh understanding of how AI applications work, with practical insights you can apply\.

## The 3\-Layer Pyramid of AI Applications

To avoid confusion, let’s structure all AI products into a 3\-layer pyramid:

1. **Core Brain \(Large Language Models \- LLM\)**

2. **Senses \& Limbs \(Multimodal Models\)**

3. **Skeleton \& Tools \(Internet, Knowledge Bases, Workflows, Intelligent Agents\)**

These three layers determine what AI can actually do for you\. Let’s dive into each layer\.

## Layer 1: The Core Brain \- Large Language Models \(LLM\)

LLMs are the “brain” of AI\. Think of them as a professor who knows everything—they can answer any question quickly and have a perfect memory\. Popular LLMs include DeepSeek, Doubao, Qwen 3, ChatGPT, and Gemini 3\.0\.

### Key Capabilities

- Process text and logical thinking\.

- Act as a central hub for understanding and generating information\.

### Critical Limitations

1. **Knowledge Limitations**

    - **Time Lag**: An LLM trained last month won’t know what happened this month\. It can’t access future data\.

    - **Scope**: LLMs don’t know personal or company\-specific data\. For example, if you ask Doubao about *your* next week’s work plan, it will make things up—because that data isn’t in its training set\.

2. **Passive Nature**
LLMs only respond when asked\. They won’t 主动 provide information unless prompted\. For example, ask it about *The Three\-Body Problem*, and it will summarize it—but it won’t offer the summary unprompted\.

### Mastering Prompts

The key to working with LLMs is writing good prompts\. A prompt’s essence is: *Don’t let the model guess who you are*\. Be specific about context, goals, and constraints\.

For example, if you want career advice, don’t just say: *“I’m confused, help me with my career plan\.”* Instead, say:

```plaintext
You are a career coach. I’m a 28-year-old marketing specialist with 5 years of experience in digital marketing. I want to transition into product management. My strengths are data analysis and user research. Please create a 1-year career transition plan, including skills to learn and job search strategies.
```

## Layer 2: Senses \& Limbs \- Multimodal Models

Multimodal models are AI’s “senses and limbs”—they let AI see, hear, speak, and create\. Think of them as giving the LLM “a painter’s hands, a director’s eyes, and a singer’s voice\.”

### What They Do

- Generate images \(e\.g\., Nano Banana Pro, Midjourney\), videos \(e\.g\., Veo 3\.1, Sora 2\), audio \(e\.g\., IndexTTS, Suno\), and digital humans \(e\.g\., HeyGen\)\.

- Enable “multimodal” capabilities, allowing AI to interact with text, images, audio, and video\.

### Limitation

Multimodal models still inherit the LLM’s flaws:

- They don’t have real\-time data or access to your personal/company information\.

- They’re still passive—they won’t act unless instructed\.

## Layer 3: Skeleton \& Tools \- Internet, Knowledge Bases, Workflows, and Intelligent Agents

This layer adds “skeleton and tools” to AI, solving its limitations in real\-time data, personalization, and proactivity\.

### 1\. Internet Search Tools

To fix the LLM’s time lag, we give it internet access\. For example, when you ask Doubao: *“What’s the news today?”* it will run a web search\. This is like “plugging the professor into the internet”—it can instantly access the latest global news\.

### 2\. Knowledge Bases

To add personal/company data, we use knowledge bases \(e\.g\., NotebookLM, IMA\)\. Imagine giving the LLM your private diary or a 10,000\-word contract\. It can read and analyze these instantly\.

For example, upload a contract to a knowledge base and ask:

```plaintext
Analyze this contract for potential risks, specifically focusing on payment terms and liability clauses.
```

The LLM will answer based on *your* actual data, not made\-up information\.

### 3\. Workflows \(Standard Operating Procedures \- SOP\)

Workflows turn AI into a “factory line worker”—they enforce consistent steps to ensure stability\.

**Example: Creating a Viral Article Workflow**

1. **Search for hot topics** on Toutiao in a specific niche\.

2. **Extract the title style** of the top 3 articles\.

3. **Imitate that style** to write an article that fits your personal brand\.

This turns “unpredictable creativity” into a “reliable assembly line\.”

### 4\. Intelligent Agents

Intelligent agents are like “project managers”—they think independently, plan, and act\. Unlike workflows \(which follow fixed steps\), agents decide *which tools to use* and *which workflows to run*\.

**Example: Planning a Trip to the Moon**
Tell the agent: *“Help me plan a trip to the Moon\.”*
The agent will:

1. Use a tool to check Moon travel “tickets\.”

2. Run a workflow to gather travel guides\.

3. Compile everything into a document and send it to you\.

Agents turn AI from a “chatbot” into a “doer\.”
